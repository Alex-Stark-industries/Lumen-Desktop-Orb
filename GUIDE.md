# Command guide

Right-click the orb to see this menu. Every command below runs instantly —
no thinking, no waiting on a model — and every reply is spoken aloud as well
as shown briefly above the orb.

Commands marked **…** ask for one line of text first (a small box appears
next to the orb — type your answer and press Enter).

**Open…** and **Weather…** autocomplete as you type — the rest of the best
match (an installed app, or a city) appears filled in and highlighted, the
same way a browser's address bar completes a URL. Press **→** or **End**,
or click past what you typed, to accept it; keep typing to ignore it.

## Info

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Time** | Speaks the current time. |
| **Date** | Speaks today's date. |
| **System status** | Speaks how much memory is in use and how long your PC has been running. |
| **Battery** | Speaks your battery percentage (laptops only — desktops without a battery get a short, honest "no battery detected"). |
| **What's Running** | Speaks the names of apps currently open with a visible window. |

## Actions

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Volume Up** | Turns your system volume up one notch — the same as your keyboard's volume key. |
| **Volume Down** | Turns your system volume down one notch. |
| **Start with Windows: On/Off** | Toggles whether Lumen launches automatically when you log in. The label always shows the current state; off by default — Lumen never turns this on by itself. |

## Launching things

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Open Notepad** | Opens Notepad. |
| **Open Calculator** | Opens the Calculator. |
| **Open…** | Type the name of any installed app (or a folder, or a web address) and Lumen tries to open it — the same way Windows' own Run box (Win+R) resolves things. If nothing by that name is found, Lumen tells you instead of staying silent. Autocompletes against your installed apps as you type. |

## Lookups

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Web Search…** | Type anything, and Lumen opens your default browser with a search for it. |
| **Weather…** | Type a city name, and Lumen speaks the current temperature and conditions. Autocompletes against real city names as you type. |
| **News Briefing** | Speaks the latest headline from NASA, the Federal Reserve, NIST, the Department of Labor, and NOAA, naming the sources at the end. See "Why only U.S. government sources?" below. |

## About

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| **About Lumen** | A short, fixed spoken introduction — what Lumen is (and isn't) — including the version number currently running, the quickest way to check an auto-update actually landed. |

## Quit

**Quit Lumen** sits at the bottom of the menu, on its own, in red. It closes
the app completely — there's currently no way to just "hide" it.

---

### Why some commands ask a question and others don't

Lumen has no language model running anywhere — every command above is a
small, fixed routine that either reads a real piece of data (the clock, your
battery) or performs a real action (opening an app, changing the volume).
The three commands that need a word or two from you (**Open…**, **Web
Search…**, **Weather…**) are the only ones where that fixed routine needs a
detail only you can provide — everything else, Lumen already knows or can
check on its own, instantly.

### Why only U.S. government sources for News Briefing?

Newspaper RSS feeds (BBC, the New York Times, and nearly every other major
outlet checked) restrict their feeds to personal, non-commercial use, which
Lumen — sold, not given away — can't honor. Works published by the U.S.
federal government carry no copyright at all (17 U.S.C. §105), so they're
free to read aloud without a license. That trade-off means a narrower topic
range — science, economic data, technology — instead of general-interest
news, but it's the one combination that's actually free to use.
